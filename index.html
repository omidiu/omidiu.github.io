I’m Omid Saghatchian. I enjoy solving complex problems. I approach them by examining the underlying assumptions, breaking them into simpler parts, and building solutions through careful reasoning.

I completed my master’s studies in computer science at Shahid Beheshti University, where I ranked first in my cohort. For my thesis, I looked for a better way to test how well language models reason about code. My supervisor was Saeed Reza Kheradpisheh. I worked with Khashayar Etemadi, a postdoctoral researcher at ETH Zurich, to develop SCRBench, a benchmark that breaks code reasoning into distinct skills and evaluates them separately. Our manuscript is under review at NAACL.

I earned my bachelor’s degree in mathematics from Amirkabir University of Technology. Toward the end of my studies, I began exploring how to speed up diffusion models while preserving output quality. I looked for computations the model could reuse across steps. That idea led to Cached Adaptive Token Merging, a method I developed with my coauthors to reduce repeated computation during inference.

Research collaboration

Under the supervision of Malikeh Ehghaghi at the Vector Institute, I explored how to assess what an attacker can learn from a safeguarded language model. I also categorized the cybersecurity questions in WMDP by topic.
